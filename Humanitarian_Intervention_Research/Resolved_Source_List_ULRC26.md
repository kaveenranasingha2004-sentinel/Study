# Source List — ULRC '26

## Common Sources

| Source | Look for |
|---|---|
| UN Charter, Arts 2(1), 2(4), 2(7) | Sovereign equality, non-intervention norm, domestic-jurisdiction carve-out and its Chapter VII exception |
| ICISS, *The Responsibility to Protect* (2001) | Original triggers for intervention, before 2005 dilution |
| 2005 World Summit Outcome Document, UNGA Res 60/1, paras 138–139 | Diluted, UNSC-gated version of R2P actually adopted |
| Montevideo Convention (1933), Art 1 | Four-criteria definition of statehood |
| Genocide Convention (1948), Art II | Legal definition of genocide |
| Rome Statute of the ICC (1998), Art 8 | Legal definition of war crimes |
| OSCOLA 5th edition | Footnote format per source type — treaty, case, UN document, book |

## Member 1 — Doctrinal Foundations of Sovereignty

| Source | Look for |
|---|---|
| Ian Brownlie, *Principles of Public International Law* | Standard doctrinal statement of sovereignty's core attributes |
| James Crawford, *The Creation of States in International Law* | How sovereignty is legally acquired via recognition practice |
| Antonio Cassese, *International Law* | Statement of the Westphalian model — territorial exclusivity, non-interference |
| *Nicaragua v United States* (ICJ 1986) | Non-intervention principle; US non-compliance and veto of enforcement afterward |
| *Island of Palmas* (Arbitration, 1928) | Classical arbitral definition of sovereignty as exclusive territorial authority |
| Andreas Osiander, 'Sovereignty, International Relations, and the Westphalian Myth' (2001) 55(2) International Organization 251 | Argument that the standard "Westphalian sovereignty" narrative is a later myth, not what the 1648 treaties actually established |

## Member 2 — Humanitarian Intervention as a Legal Regime

| Source | Look for |
|---|---|
| Simon Chesterman, *Just War or Just Peace?* | Treatment of state practice as inconsistent evidence for customary law |

| Independent International Commission on Kosovo, *The Kosovo Report* (2000) | "Illegal but legitimate" formulation |
| Anne Orford, *Reading Humanitarian Intervention* | How humanitarian intervention discourse constructs its own legitimacy |

## Member 3 — Sri Lanka / Global South Case Study + Recommendations

| Source | Look for |
|---|---|
| UN Panel of Experts on Accountability in Sri Lanka ("Darusman Report"), 31 March 2011 | Findings on shelling of "no-fire zones," denial of humanitarian access; Sri Lankan government's rejection of the panel on sovereignty grounds |
| Lessons Learnt and Reconciliation Commission (LLRC) Report, Sri Lanka (2011) | Findings and legitimacy claims, read against the Darusman Report |
| UNHRC Resolutions 30/1 (2015), 40/1 (2019), 46/1 (2021) | Co-sponsorship and withdrawal by Sri Lanka; mandate strength across the three, esp. 46/1's evidence-gathering mechanism |
| Antony Anghie, *Imperialism, Sovereignty and the Making of International Law* | Sovereignty doctrine constructed through the colonial encounter |
| B.S. Chimni, TWAIL scholarship on selective intervention | International law legitimising, not merely permitting, Global North intervention |
| Stephen Krasner, *Sovereignty: Organized Hypocrisy* (1999) | "Organized hypocrisy" thesis — sovereignty norms routinely violated when convenient |
| Libya (2011), comparative case | UNSC 1973 mandate mechanics (with Member 2); aftermath — Russia/China blocking Syria action |
| *Application of the Genocide Convention (South Africa v Israel)*, ICJ Orders of 26 Jan 2024, 28 Mar 2024, 24 May 2024 | Genocide Convention/ICJ route as procedurally available but slow, non-dispositive |
